**Hi, I'm Moriel.**

By day (and, honestly, a fair number of nights), I think about software: how teams build it, how they ship it, and why it so often goes sideways anyway. I write about that on [my blog](https://blog.moriel.tech) and talk about it on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@MorielTech), including a series that takes a real law of physics and asks what it says about your codebase. Because before there was software, there was physics. I studied it, I still love it, and I can't seem to stop bringing it up.

This site is for the other kind of writing: stories, flash fiction, and the occasional poem. Some started as writing-workshop exercises, some as replies to other people's poems and stories, and a few just showed up one day and refused to leave.

They aren't all good. Maybe some are. I'm not sure. I just figured this is a better place for them than a dusty drawer.

## Why "Imaginary Parts"?

It's a little bit of a physics joke. Complex numbers have a real part and an imaginary part, and physics leans on the imaginary one all the time: quantum mechanics is built on it, and it shows up wherever something waves, rings, or fades away. It's the part that supposedly doesn't exist, and it quietly does a lot of the work.

It's also exactly what these are: the imaginary parts of me. My day job is the real part; this is where the made-up bits get to run around.

## The rest of me

When I'm not writing (code or otherwise), I'm probably reading or watching sci-fi, listening to an audiobook, rambling, debating, philosophizing, or generally dorking out about something.

Prefer to listen? Every piece has a **Listen** button that reads it to you.

## Tell me what you think

I take this writing seriously, and honest feedback is the best way to get better. If a piece worked for you, I'd love to hear it. If something didn't, I'd love to hear that even more. [Drop me a note](https://moriel.tech/contact). I promise not to cry.

At least not immediately.
